3. Game Loop & Time MechanicsThe simulation operates on a continuous real-time tick during working hours, transitioning to a turn-based summary at the end of the day.Time Scale: 1 real second = 1 in-game minute.Operating Hours: 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM (12 real minutes per active day).End-of-Day (EOD) Phase: At 8:00 PM, the continuous tick pauses. The game resolves overnight mechanics (complete wood drying, daily fixed-cost deductions, loan interest) and presents the financial dashboard before the player initiates the next day.4. Economic & Production ModelsSpatial ConstraintsEvery asset requires physical space. Deliveries fail if the property cannot house the materials.$$\text{Available Storage} = \text{Total SqFt} - \sum \text{Equipment Footprint} - \sum \text{Active Drying Racks}$$Worker Progression and OutputLabor efficiency scales non-linearly. To simulate realistic skill acquisition curves, reaching mastery takes exponentially more time than learning the basics.$$\text{XP}_{\text{req}} = \text{Base}_{\text{XP}} \times (\text{Level})^{1.5}$$Hourly production is bottlenecked by the physical speed of the worker relative to the maximum capacity of their assigned equipment.$$\text{Hourly Output} = \text{Equip}_{\text{Max}} \times \left( \frac{\text{Worker}_{\text{Speed}}}{100} \right)$$Quality Control & Material WasteSloppy execution directly reduces profit margins. A worker's quality stat determines the percentage of ruined inventory.$$\text{Waste Percentage} = \text{Machine}_{\text{Base Error}} + \left( \frac{100 - \text{Worker}_{\text{Quality}}}{200} \right)$$Market Pricing & Mill RelationshipsPlayers buffer against global commodity price spikes by building localized reputation with specific lumber mills, securing bulk discounts.$$\text{Final Cost} = \text{Market Price} \times \left( 1 - \left( \frac{\text{Reputation}}{100} \right) \times 0.20 \right)$$5. Logistics & Fleet ManagementScaling the business requires expanding beyond static warehouses into active transportation networks. Managing OTR truck selection, container routes, and driver leasing logistics forms the core of the mid-to-late game.Toll Contracts: Customers deliver wood to the player's yard (1 to 2 in-game hours). Low financial risk, low margin.Purchase Contracts: Players buy raw lumber directly from mills (4 to 6 in-game hours). High capital requirement, high margin.Fleet Mechanics: Players can eliminate mill delivery fees by purchasing their own vehicles.Heavy-Duty Pickups: Low capacity, requires standard drivers, high frequency of trips.Flatbeds & Semi-Trucks: High capacity, requires expensive CDL drivers, heavy insurance premiums, and maintenance tracking. Vehicle breakdowns act as a severe disruption to the daily production queue.6. Financial SystemsFinancial friction is engineered to prevent players from easily gaming the labor or real estate markets.Hiring & Firing: Hiring requires upfront marketing capital (Job Postings). Firing incurs a massive immediate severance penalty (10 to 14 times the worker's daily wage), forcing players to strategize whether to pay idle workers during a lull or take the immediate cash hit.Capitalization: Players define their starting difficulty (e.g., $50,000 cash with no debt vs. $100,000 cash with a high-interest loan).Real Estate Expansion: Players must balance cheap, undeveloped acreage (Storage Lots) used solely for stockpiling inventory against expensive, commercially zoned buildings (Production Yards) required for operating heavy machinery.
